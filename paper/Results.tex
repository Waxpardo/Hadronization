\subsection{Balancing of beauty and charm hadrons}
\label{sec:results:balancing}
The integrated yield plots are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:secResults:GlobalYieldsBBCC} for B$^{+}$ and prompt D$^{+}$ meson trigger and appropriate associate particles as function of multiplicity. Focusing first on the beauty case, a distinct increase is seen in baryon balancing partners when switching on the Junctions tune compared with the Monash baseline. This increase from Monash to Junctions seems to be only weakly dependent on multiplicity, though the yield itself increases for larger multiplicity due to the increased number of MPIs, allowing the Junction mechanism to perform better as there will be a larger chance that different MPI states overlap in the same phase space. It should be noted that the increase in baryon yield for Junctions is especially strong for $\Sigma_{b}$ baryon associates: an order of magnitude larger than the increase in the almost identical $\Lambda^{0}_{b}$ baryons. This is explained by the fact that the $uu$ and $dd$ di-quarks are necessary to create the $\Sigma_{b}^{+/-}$ baryons and can only exist in a isospin-1 state, while the $\Sigma_{b}^{0}$ baryon contains a $ud$ di-quark that can reside in the much more energetically favoured isospin-0 state. This suppression is lifted when CR mechanisms are at play as they allow hadronisation without spin dependence. The $\Sigma_{b}$ baryons are therefore created with equal probabilities and this is reflected in Fig.~\ref{fig:secResults:GlobalYieldsBBCC}. Both the Monash and Junctions tunes apply CR mechanisms, but the beyond leading colour treatment in the latter enhances this production in $\Sigma_{b}$ baryons even more. \\
% Besides the CR and isospin effects, there also seems to be a strong dependence on the particle mass for the baryon balancing partners, as the mass difference in $\Sigma_{b}$ and $\Lambda_{b}$ is only about 7\% [check]. 
\indent Turning to the charm sector, the trends seen in the beauty balancing results are mostly reproduced for charm. One of the differences is the substantial multiplicity dependence of the Junctions enhancement ratio for the $\overline{\Sigma_{c}}$ baryons, while the $\Sigma_{b}$ baryons ratios were almost constant as a function of multiplicity. This seems to be related to how PYTHIA treats differences in $\Sigma_{b}$ and $\overline{\Sigma_{c}}$ baryons as the effect is not seen for the $\Lambda^{0}_{b}$ and $\overline{\Lambda^{+}_{c}}$ baryons. \\
% There have not been many measurements of $\Sigma_{b}$ baryons, which hints that the tuning of PYTHIA can also be related to the difference seen in multiplicity dependence. \\
\indent Another difference can be seen in the enhanced production of $\overline{\Lambda^{+}_{c}}$ baryons in Junctions, which is less pronounced than the equivalent beauty case. This again hints at differences in beauty and charm within PYTHIA and does not seem to be related only to mass effects. An explicit example of how this difference manifests is found in the \texttt{StringFlav:ProbQQ1toQQ0join} parameter (\href{https://pythia.org/latest-manual/FlavourSelection.html}{PYTHIA:FlavourSelection}). This parameter controls the suppression of spin-1 di-quarks production relative to spin-0 when two quarks are combined to a di-quark in the Junctions framework. The default value (for Monash) when the heaviest quark is charm is $0.9$ and $1.0$ for beauty, giving an example of differences in beauty and charm implementation that can be argued to be causing the observed differences. The parameter has identical values in the Junctions tune, which is why this only explains differences in Monash. Some caution is required in the discussion: there seems to be some disagreement with data when the default setting is used and this is being tuned \cite{StringFlavPaper}, however, and as there is no data available for the equivalent beauty case. Specifically, equalising the beauty and charm values for the \texttt{StringFlav:ProbQQ1toQQ0join} setting is not recommended and therefore not pursued.

\subsection{Baryon/meson balancing ratio of beauty and charm hadrons}
\label{sec:results:b/mBalancing}
The baryon/meson balancing ratios are given in Fig.~\ref{fig:secResults:GlobalRelYieldsBBCC} for B$^{+}$ and prompt $D^{-}$ trigger particles for different associate baryons as a function of multiplicity. The relative $\Lambda_{b}^{0}$ and $\overline{\Lambda_{c}^{+}}$ ratios are treated separately from the combined case including the $\Sigma_{b}^{0}$ and $\overline{\Sigma^{+}_{c}}$ baryons as the difference in yields differ up to an order of magnitude from each other. \\
\indent A distinct increasing trend is observed in the baryon/meson ratio with increasing multiplicity in the Junctions tune for roughly all considered balancing partners. This is to be expected as the events with higher multiplicity are created through an enhanced number of MPIs, suspected to create more junction topologies in the hadronisation process, increasing the number of baryon partners. This trend is broken however in the higher multiplicity bins, where the ratio actually decreases. For beauty this occurs only in the highest multiplicity interval, but for charm this behaviour sets in already at mid-multiplicities in the 20-40 multiplicity bin due to the difference in mass. \\
\indent Beside the differences in multiplicity dependence, there are clear differences between beauty and (prompt) charm, while naively it is expected that the prompt nature of the charm particles will cancel any differences with the beauty counterparts. The ratio seems to be particularly different among the considered particles for the Monash tune which hints at differences in the treatment of beauty and charm in PYTHIA instead of differences caused by the beyond leading colour mechanisms or originating from a physical origin. This belief is enhanced by the fact that the \texttt{StringFlav:ProbQQ1toQQ0join} parameter has different values for beauty and charm in Monash, but not in Junctions.
