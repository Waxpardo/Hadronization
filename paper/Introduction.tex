\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The conversion of coloured partons into colour-neutral hadrons is a
non-perturbative part of quantum chromodynamics. The Lund string model provides
a phenomenological description in which the colour field fragments through
quark--antiquark and diquark--antidiquark production
\cite{Andersson:1983ia}. PYTHIA~8 combines this model with hard scattering,
parton showers, multiparton interactions, and model-dependent colour
rearrangements \cite{Bierlich:2022pfr}.

Heavy flavour offers a particularly direct way to ask where a conserved
quantum number goes during hadronisation. A selected hard charm or beauty quark
is carried by one final heavy hadron. Its compensating antiquark quantum number
must be carried elsewhere in the event, possibly by a meson or a baryon and at
a characteristic angular separation from the trigger. This question is not
answered by an inclusive baryon-to-meson production ratio: it requires an
ordered, trigger-conditioned heavy-flavour correlation.\claim{I1}

The analysis compares three complete PYTHIA configurations. MONASH supplies
the baseline tune \cite{Skands:2014pea}; JUNCTIONS includes the beyond-leading-
colour configuration associated with junction topologies
\cite{Christiansen:2015yqa}; and CLOSEPACKING includes a dense-string
close-packing configuration \cite{Altmann:2025afh}. The cards differ in 28
allowed settings across nine families.\claim{I2} Consequently, every
difference reported here is a bundle-to-bundle comparison. The present samples
do not vary junction formation, close packing, colour reconnection, string
flavour production, or any other mechanism independently.

We measure opposite- and same-heavy-sign correlations as functions of
relative azimuth and event activity. Their difference identifies the net
heavy-flavour compensation associated with the selected trigger. Unlike-sign
minus like-sign identified-particle correlations provide nearby experimental
context \cite{Graczykowski:2014jqa}, although the forced hard-heavy generator
sample and the heavy-flavour-sign convention make the observable here
distinct.

Section~\ref{sec:observables} defines the selections, signs, normalization, and
statistical estimators. Section~\ref{sec:model} describes the campaign and the
tune-local percentile axes. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports only claims that can
be traced to committed numerical evidence and separates those claims from the
still-blocked publication figures.
