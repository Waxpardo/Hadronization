## 1. IT HAS NO POOL, AND THAT IS NOT A TUNABLE

The brief asks for a pool at P = 4–8, sampled and re-derived. **There is no P.**

`validate_analysis_outputs.py` takes `canonical_manifest`, `analysis_root`,
`--production-root`, `--checkout`, `--report`, `--allow-missing`. There is no
concurrency argument, and adding one is not a local change:

- **`:570-576` writes a single report** that `merge_root_files.sh:80-84`
  consumes. Sharded runs produce N reports and no contract for combining them.
- **The whole-set checks are meaningless over a shard.** `:371-383` computes
  `staging`, `extras` and `missing` against the *entire* expected directory set;
  `:535-537` accumulates `analysis_commits`, `macro_hashes` and
  `stability_hashes` and requires each to be single-valued **across all 3000**.
  A shard cannot evaluate either.

This was already recorded in `HANDOFF_20260806_v9.md:118` — *"has no pool and
cannot be sharded without changing a contract"* — and is re-verified here rather
than inherited. **The gate runs serially, as one process.**

**The RSS result is the striking one.** Across 25 independent directories the

essentially constant per directory**, which is what one would expect from a
fixed 300-file contract, and it means the number needs no safety margin.

**The wall result invalidates my own prediction's basis.** The two slowest runs
happened to be the first two. Serial projection on VPD alone:
**21.2 h at the mean**, 32.3 h if every directory were the worst case. The gate's
per-directory unit adds `directory_digest()` on top, so **G1's 38–48 s and G2's
32–40 h are likely to miss high** unless the digest costs ~13 s/directory.

**I am not revising G1/G2.** They were registered and they stand; recording the
better estimate separately is what keeps the prediction honest. **This is the
third time this arc that I have projected from an unrepresentative early
sample** — the 50-input scaling wall, the per-file rate direction, and now this.
The pattern is specific and worth naming: *early samples in this environment run
slow, because caches are cold and the first jobs land on whatever node is
free.*

### PROGRESS PROBE — the gate IS observable, and my "no progress signal" claim was wrong

**v33 §2 and v34 said the gate emits nothing until it finishes and that a quiet
log is the only signal. That was wrong.** There is a precise progress signal; it
is simply not in the log.

**`/data/alice` is mounted `relatime`**, so the first read of a file after it was
written stamps its `atime`. The gate walks directories in manifest order, so
**`atime > mtime` marks exactly how far it has got**:

> ## ⚠ TWO CORRECTIONS TO THE METHOD BELOW — both found by measurement
>
> **1. The comparison is `atime > job_start`, NOT `atime > mtime`.** The
> unfiltered form detects *any* read and will report your own earlier probes as
> gate progress. (Recorded in §4's ruling table; repeated here because this is
> where the method is actually read.)
>
> **2. The frontier is the MAXIMUM INDEX, never the COUNT.** Measured
> 2026-08-11 03:30: **934 directories carried a fresh atime while the frontier
> was slot_972** — a 38-directory shortfall. Under `relatime` a directory whose
> atime is already ≥ its mtime and less than 24 h old is **not re-stamped**, so
> any directory touched by an earlier pass is invisible to the count.
> `slot_970` still carried a 19:00 atime from a prior read while 969, 971 and
> 972 were fresh.
>
> **Counting stamped directories undercounts progress. Take the maximum index.**

> ## ⛔ THIRD CORRECTION 2026-08-12 — the probe is INVALID on MERGED outputs
>
> **The atime frontier does not work on the merged directories at all**, and it
> fails silently by reporting **zero progress**.
>
> Measured while MONASH closure was 7.3 h into a run: `atime > start` returned
> **0 of 300 files in all eleven directories**, while `/proc/<pid>/fd` showed the
> process holding files in `combined_root_4` and `combined_root_5` **open and
> being read at that moment**, with `read_bytes` climbing 2.9 MB in 40 s.
>
> **Why:** `relatime` updates atime only when the previous atime is older than
> mtime, or older than 24 h. Every merged pair file was **written and then read
> by the merge's own `ValidatePairDirectory` pass** before promotion, so its
> atime already equals mtime and is minutes old. A subsequent read updates
> nothing.
>
> **The probe's validity condition is that the files have NOT been read
> recently.** That holds for `per_job/` inputs during the gate — which is where
> it was measured and where it works — and is **violated by construction** for
> anything the merge has just produced.
>
> **Use instead, for merged outputs:** `/proc/<pid>/fd` (names the file being
> read *now*, giving position directly) and `/proc/<pid>/io` `rchar`
> (monotonic, unaffected by relatime **or** page cache). Both are
> workload-intrinsic.
>
> **A zero from the atime probe is not evidence of a stall.** It nearly read as
> one here.

> ## ⛔ FOURTH CORRECTION 2026-08-12 — this section named the WRONG replacement
>
> The instrument above originally read **`read_bytes`**. That is wrong, and it
> would have failed the same silent-zero way one correction later. `read_bytes`
> counts **block-layer** fetches; `/data/alice` is **NFS4**, and the MONASH
> closure's `read_bytes` sat at **16 KB across 8.5 h** while `rchar` reached
> **16.68 GB**. The "2.9 MB in 40 s" quoted above was read during the window in
> which that session records having had the merge and closure PIDs **swapped** —
> it is the merge's counter, which is non-zero because the merge stages through
> local `/tmp`.
>
> **`rchar` is the progress counter. `read_bytes` answers cache-vs-disk, and on
> NFS answers it as zero.**

> ## 📄 THE METHOD NOW LIVES IN `docs/PROGRESS_PROBE_METHOD.md`
>
> This section is kept as the **measurement record**. The consolidated method —
> decision tree, validity condition per instrument, and how to turn an `fd`
> position into a *validated* fraction (take the unit order from the code, not
> from a directory listing) — is maintained there. Two further corrections are
> recorded in it: that `rchar` is a **step function** whose short-interval
> flatness is not a stall, and that a validated position still yields **no ETA**
> under non-uniform unit costs.

```

slot_005  21:31:34      slot_055  21:49:45      (slot_105+ unread)

```

### The CPU probe returned a FALSE NEGATIVE — amend the convention

The brief's new convention is *sample process CPU twice ~60 s apart; flat =
investigate*. Applied here:

```
sample1  RemoteUserCpu=2554.0 RemoteSysCpu=997.0
sample2 (+150 s)  2554.0  997.0      flat
sample3 (+400 s)  2554.0             STILL FLAT
```

**Flat across 550 s on a job that was demonstrably executing a directory every
22 s.** The cause is that `condor_q` reads the *schedd's copy* of the job ad,
refreshed on the startd's update cycle (default `STARTER_UPDATE_INTERVAL` 300 s,
and the schedd-side propagation is slower still). **CPU-via-`condor_q` measures
ad freshness, not liveness.**

> **Amendment, and it is why "flat = investigate" rather than "flat = dead" was
> the right wording:** for a silent job, prefer a **workload-intrinsic** progress
> signal over a scheduler-reported one. Here that is the `atime` frontier. Where
> no such signal exists, sample CPU over **≥ 3× the update interval** (≥ 900 s)
> before treating flatness as evidence of anything — and note that accumulated
> CPU/wall (here 3551 s / 4728 s = **0.751**, matching the 0.799 measured on
> single directories) is itself corroboration that the job has been working.

**Verdict: the gate is ALIVE and on schedule. It will outlive this session.**

## 5. TWO INSTRUCTIONS THAT WERE WRONG, AND WHY THE RECORD KEEPS THEM

Both came down as directions to follow; both were refused with evidence; both
refusals were upheld. They are kept because the *shape* of each error recurs.

### 5a. "Size the gate's pool at P = 4–8"

**A category error, and the record already contained its refutation.**
`validate_analysis_outputs.py` has no pool and cannot acquire one without
breaking a contract (§1). **`RELEASE_BLOCKERS.md` B10 calls it "the serial
checksum gate" in its own heading** — the serialism was not a discovery, it was
written down and not consulted.

**The lesson is about instructions, not about pools:** an instruction that
presumes a capability should be checked against the record before it is
executed, because the record may already say the capability does not exist.

### 5b. "The 441 MiB figure is superseded"

**A cross-attribution.** Two different programs were treated as one:

| program | what it does | figure |
|---|---|---|
| `ValidatePairDirectory` (gate) | opens 300 pair files, validates | **442.3 MiB ± 0.9 %** — *confirmed* |
| `MergeCanonicalAnalysis.C` (merge) | merges N inputs into 300 files | 571 → **657 MiB** — *superseded* |

The v3 scaling series measured **only** the merge. Applying its result to the
gate would have replaced a correct number with an unrelated one — and the
replacement would have looked like diligence.

**The lesson:** "re-derive everything from the new measurement" is safe only
when the new measurement covers everything. Here it covered one of two programs,
and the instruction's scope was wider than the evidence's.

### 5c. STANDING CAVEAT — the early-sample mechanism

Ratified as convention after three consecutive instances (the 50-input wall, the
per-file rate's direction, this gate's per-directory wall):

> **Early samples in this environment run slow.** Cold caches, and first jobs
> land on whatever node is free.
>
> - **Sizing pre-registrations quote their `n`.**
> - **Bands revise on record at `n ≥ 10`** — revised openly, beside the
>   original, never by overwriting it.
> - A projection from `n < 10` is an estimate with a direction, not a band.

### 5d. STANDING CONVENTION — escalation urgency matches decision urgency

Ratified after the 100-input anchor, where I escalated twice from partial data
and was wrong both times.

> **A blocking conclusion issues from a settled number, unless a deadline forces
> otherwise.** Where nothing can act on the alarm before the number settles, the
> correct form is **"flag now, conclude at the sentinel"** — not *"X must not
> launch."*
>
> **Any escalation from partial data carries its expiry stamp:** *"this moves;
> it settles at ~3 h."* An escalation without one invites a decision the
> evidence cannot yet support.

**The concrete case:** nothing could have acted on the n=21 alarm — the merge was
gated behind a 21–33 h gate PASS regardless — so *"the merge must not launch"*
bought nothing and cost credibility. **"Flag now, conclude at the sentinel"** was
available and free.

**Note what the system did, though:** the conditional GO meant the wrong
conclusion never touched anything. The merge was gated on a PASS that had not
happened, so a false alarm and a true one had identical effect on the world.
**Defense in depth absorbed it by design** — which is the argument for keeping
conditional gating even when it feels redundant.

### 5e. STANDING CONVENTION — assertions pin the program, not the environment

Ratified after cluster `5399189` aborted on `HEAD == 61fe978f`, an assertion
that was correct when written and expired silently when the freeze lifted.

> **A comparability assertion pins the PROGRAM — the macro sha256 — never the
> environment.** Checkout HEAD, hostname, and paths are not what makes two
> measurements comparable; the code that produced them is.

- **Right:** `sha256sum merging/MergeCanonicalAnalysis.C` compared to a
  recorded constant, as `scaling_100_anchor.sh` does.
- **Wrong:** `git rev-parse HEAD` compared to a pinned commit, as
  `scaling_1025_batch.sh` did. It passes for the wrong reason while the freeze
  holds, and fails for the wrong reason afterwards.

**The tell:** ask what the assertion protects against. If the answer is "the code
changing underneath me", assert the code. **An environment assertion is a proxy,
and proxies expire.**

**Where this applies:** every scratch measurement script, and any comparison that
will be quoted against an earlier number.
