## 1. RETRACTION — an overclaim of mine, corrected by owner ruling

I wrote in the campaign record that the true discard bias was "far smaller"
than the bound, because a wedged job produces no events, so **"a wedge is a lost
draw, not a skewed one."** **That is retracted, and the per-tune bounds stand as
the absent-event-class worst case.**

**Why it does not follow.** The argument shows a wedged job contributes no
*biased events*. It says nothing about whether **the set of jobs that wedge** is
correlated with event content — which is the actual open question, and which I
assumed rather than established.

**The decisive fact, which I did not have:** the hang mechanism sits in
**`JunctionSplitting`** — plausibly correlated with exactly the junction
topologies this study measures, and consistent with MONASH 0 % against both CR
tunes ~6 %.

**What the lottery evidence really shows.** A fresh seed reproduces ~6 %, so the
hang is not seed-specific. That is **consistent with** content-independence but
does **not establish** it: a content-correlated mechanism firing on ~6 % of
*event sequences* would look identical under fresh draws. **The retry data does
not distinguish the two hypotheses.**

The bimodality result stands on its own claim — genuine wedges, guard never
mis-fired. **It carries no implication about discard bias.** I over-extended it.
