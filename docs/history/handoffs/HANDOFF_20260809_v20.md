**Widening `hFlavourClosure` in place** (adding a 202-bin species axis to the
existing object) avoids the contract entirely. **I rejected it on measured
grounds:**

- `hFlavourClosure` carried **124,494 filled bins at 100 inputs** (measured).

  populate it — plausibly 5–20x.
- **It is the object whose merge cost produced the 49x cliff, and that cliff's
  mechanism is UNKNOWN** — chunking was falsified in v8 and nothing replaced it.
  **A cost that cannot be attributed cannot be extrapolated**, so the bin
  inflation cannot be bounded in advance.
- **The merge budget was settled only three sessions ago** (B10, both models),
  and a live 3000-job campaign is running against it.

**Changing the cost profile of the merge, during the campaign, by an amount
nobody can predict, to avoid asking for one config edit, is the wrong trade.**

**Add `hFlavourClosureSpecies` to `config/pair_file_object_contract_v1.json`
and regenerate the header via `tools/generate_registry_artifacts.py`** — one
scoped `config/` edit, the same shape as B6's deferred authorization.

ready: the **202-ordinal artifact is ratified** (`bec170f`), the **fixture is
verified byte-identical** (`49657c2c…3651`), and the four sites are sized

precedent to follow is `status_analysis_THnSparse_qq.C:1157-1163`
(`associate_origin_category_labels`).

**Design note for that session:** the parallel-object shape (species replacing

validation a *genuine* cross-check — the species→category mapping is derived
independently of the producer's `heavyStateCategory`, so summing one and
