## 1. THE GATE IS OBSERVABLE — and my "no progress signal" claim was wrong

**v33 §2 and v34 both said the gate emits nothing until it finishes and that a
quiet log is the only signal. That was wrong**, and it mattered — it would have
left a successor unable to distinguish a healthy 19-hour job from a wedged one.

**`/data/alice` is mounted `relatime`.** The first read of a file after it was
written stamps its `atime`, and the gate walks directories in manifest order, so
**`atime > mtime` marks exactly how far it has got.** The frontier is dead
regular:

```
slot_000 21:29:44   slot_050 21:47:57   slot_100 22:06:38   ... slot_320 later
every 5 slots, ~109 s apart
```

| | measured |
|---|---|
| **rate** | **22.14 s/directory** (100 dirs in 2214 s) |
| pre-loop phase | **49.2 min** — the raw-input verification pass, before any directory |
| **total projected** | **19.27 h**, ETA **2026-08-10 ~15:56** |

**Against the pre-registration:** **G1 (38–48 s/dir) and G2 (32–40 h) both MISS
LOW** — actual 22.14 s and 19.27 h. **§3b predicted exactly this**, and the n=25
revision (~21 h) called it **within 8 %**. That is the `n ≥ 10` convention
earning its keep: the registered numbers came from n=2 and were 1.7–2.1× too
pessimistic, and the revision was recorded *before* the run without ever being
substituted for the registration.

### The CPU probe returned a FALSE NEGATIVE — the new convention needs amending

The brief's new convention: *sample process CPU twice ~60 s apart; flat =
investigate.* Applied here:

```
sample1            RemoteUserCpu=2554.0  RemoteSysCpu=997.0
sample2 (+150 s)   2554.0  997.0     flat
sample3 (+400 s)   2554.0            STILL FLAT
```

**Flat across 550 s on a job demonstrably completing a directory every 22 s.**
`condor_q` reads the **schedd's copy** of the ad, refreshed on the startd update
cycle (default `STARTER_UPDATE_INTERVAL` 300 s, slower to propagate).
**CPU-via-`condor_q` measures ad freshness, not liveness.**

> **Amendment (recorded in `docs/GATE_3000.md` §4):** for a silent job, prefer a
> **workload-intrinsic** progress signal over a scheduler-reported one — here the
> `atime` frontier. Where none exists, sample over **≥ 3× the update interval
> (≥ 900 s)** before treating flatness as evidence. And note that accumulated
> CPU/wall (**0.751**, against 0.799 measured on single directories) is itself
> corroboration that the job has been working.

**"flat = investigate" rather than "flat = dead" is what saved this** — the
convention's wording was right even though its instrument was wrong.
